Does Switch 2 need a screen protector?

The Switch 2's screen is not scratch-resistant glass like Gorilla Glass on a flagship phone — it's standard LCD panel glass that scratches with everyday contact. Keys in a pocket, grit in a carrying case, and surface contact during handheld play all leave marks over time. A screen protector costs $8–$20 and prevents damage that costs hundreds to fix.

More practically: the Switch 2 is a handheld device that goes into bags, gets passed between players, and rests on surfaces. The OLED screen on Switch 1 was notoriously easy to scratch. The Switch 2's larger LCD panel is no different. Put a protector on it the day you unbox it — replacing it later is more work than doing it right the first time.

📐 Switch 2 screen size is 7.9 inches — larger than the Switch OLED's 7-inch screen. Switch 1 and OLED screen protectors will not fit. Always buy a protector specifically designed for Switch 2.

Tempered glass vs film — which should you buy?

🛡️

Tempered glass — best for most

Rigid glass layer bonded to the screen. Absorbs impacts, resists scratches, and maintains the screen's original feel and clarity. The standard choice for good reason.

  • Scratch and impact protection
  • Preserves original touch feel
  • Easy to clean — oils wipe off
  • Optically clear — no haziness
  • Easy bubble-free installation
  • Can crack on hard direct impact
  • Adds slight thickness at edges
📄

Film — best for edge cases

Thin flexible plastic film that conforms to the screen. Excellent scratch protection, self-healing on minor marks, and covers curved areas glass can't reach.

  • Flexible — covers full screen edge
  • Self-healing on minor scratches
  • Full-body options available
  • Thinner profile than glass
  • Harder to install bubble-free
  • Touch feel slightly different
  • Less impact protection than glass
  • Attracts fingerprints more
💡 Our recommendation: tempered glass for almost everyone. The touch feel is identical to the bare screen, installation is straightforward with an alignment frame, and it provides meaningfully better impact protection. Film is only worth considering if you want full-body coverage or need a protector that wraps around curved screen edges.

Installation guide — how to apply without bubbles

Bubbles are almost always caused by dust or rushing. Follow these steps and you'll get it right on the first try.

1

Pick the right environment

Do this in a bathroom right after running the shower — the steam settles dust particles out of the air. Alternatively, find the least dusty room in your house and wipe down the surrounding surfaces first. Avoid carpeted rooms where fibers float constantly.

2

Power off and clean the screen thoroughly

Turn the Switch 2 off completely. Use the included alcohol wipe to clean the screen — wipe in one direction, not circular. Follow with the microfiber cloth. Use the included dust sticker to lift any remaining particles. Hold the Switch 2 up to a light source and inspect carefully before proceeding.

3

Use the alignment frame if included

Many premium screen protectors include a plastic alignment frame that clips to the Switch 2 and positions the protector perfectly before you lay it down. Use it — alignment is the biggest source of off-center installations. If your protector doesn't include a frame, use the camera hole or speaker grille as reference points for alignment before letting it drop.

4

Peel and place — don't slide

Peel back the first adhesive layer and position the protector above the screen without touching the adhesive side. When aligned, lower it straight down — don't slide it into position once contact is made. Let gravity and the adhesive do the work. Most tempered glass protectors self-adhere without pressing.

5

Press out any bubbles from center to edge

Use the included squeegee card or your thumb wrapped in the microfiber cloth. Start from the center and push firmly outward in straight lines toward each edge. Most air bubbles will disappear immediately. Small residual bubbles at the edges usually disappear on their own within 24–48 hours.

6

Deal with dust bubbles

If a dust particle got trapped and created a bubble, don't panic. Use the included dust sticker (or a folded piece of scotch tape) to lift the corner of the protector nearest the bubble, slide the dust sticker under to grab the particle, then re-lay that section. Tempered glass is forgiving — it can be lifted and repositioned a few times before the adhesive weakens.

What to avoid

✗ Switch 1 or OLED screen protectors

Switch 2's screen is 7.9 inches — larger than both Switch 1 and OLED. Old protectors will be too small, leaving exposed screen edges and misaligned cutouts. Always confirm Switch 2 compatibility before buying.

✗ Unbranded multi-packs with no brand name

10-pack screen protectors for $4 use low-quality glass with inconsistent adhesive. They frequently arrive scratched, miscut, or with adhesive that bubbles immediately. The $3 savings is not worth the installation frustration.

✗ Liquid screen protectors

Liquid screen protectors marketed for Switch are ineffective for the use case — they add minimal scratch resistance and zero impact protection. They're not removable, can't be replaced if damaged, and cost more than a solid tempered glass option. Skip entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Does a screen protector affect touch sensitivity?
Tempered glass protectors have negligible effect on touch sensitivity — Nintendo's LCD touchscreen responds normally through 0.3mm of quality glass. Film protectors have a slightly more noticeable effect on swipe sensitivity. Both are fully functional.

What if my screen protector cracks?
A cracked screen protector means it did its job — it absorbed an impact that would have gone to your screen. Replace it promptly. A cracked protector's broken edges can scratch the screen they're protecting. That's why buying a 2-pack or 3-pack makes sense.

Can I install a screen protector with a case on?
Apply the screen protector first, before the case. This ensures a clean edge seal without the case interfering. Once the protector is down, fit your case around it — all carrying cases on our list accommodate a screen protector without issues.

How do I remove an old screen protector?
Gently lift one corner with a fingernail or thin plastic card. Pull slowly at a low angle — never straight up, which can leave adhesive residue. If residue remains, the included alcohol wipe from your new protector package will remove it cleanly.

Is the Nintendo Switch 2 screen pre-protected from the factory?
The Switch 2 ships with a thin plastic film from the factory — this is just temporary transit protection, not a proper screen protector. Remove it when you apply your permanent screen protector.
