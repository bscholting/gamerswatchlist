Do Nintendo Switch Games Work on Switch 2?

The short answer is yes — almost your entire library carries over. Here's exactly what's compatible, what gets enhanced, and the handful of exceptions worth knowing before you upgrade.

⚡ Quick Answer

Yes. The Nintendo Switch 2 is backward compatible with the vast majority of Nintendo Switch game cards and digital titles. Your existing physical cartridges slot right in. Your digital library transfers over through your Nintendo Account. Your save data moves with you.

There are a small number of exceptions — a handful of titles that rely on features the Switch 2 doesn't support. We cover every exception below. For 99% of your library, it just works.

How backward compatibility works on Switch 2

Nintendo built the Switch 2 to accept the same game card format as the original Switch. Physical cartridges from Switch 1 fit into the Switch 2's card slot and launch normally. There's no conversion process, no extra download, no compatibility mode to enable — you insert the card and press play.

For digital games, your entire Nintendo eShop library is tied to your Nintendo Account. When you log into your account on Switch 2, every digital game you've ever purchased appears in your library and can be re-downloaded. If you had a 200-game digital library on Switch 1, all 200 games are waiting for you on Switch 2.

📦 Physical games: Insert your Switch 1 cartridge into Switch 2. Done.
💾 Digital games: Log into your Nintendo Account on Switch 2. All purchases reappear automatically.

What about save data?

Save data doesn't live on the game card — it's stored on the console. To bring your saves from Switch 1 to Switch 2, you'll use Nintendo's built-in data transfer tool. It moves everything: save files, screenshots, videos, user settings, and your downloaded game library links.

Nintendo Switch Online subscribers get an additional safety net: cloud save backup. Most games automatically back up save data to Nintendo's servers, so even if you start fresh on Switch 2, your cloud saves can be re-downloaded to pick up exactly where you left off.

Are there any Switch games that don't work on Switch 2?

Yes — a small number. Nintendo has officially confirmed that games relying on certain accessories or features that Switch 2 doesn't support will not be compatible. The list is short and mostly affects niche titles:

Known incompatible titles / features

  • ✗ Games requiring the Labo cardboard accessories (IR camera-dependent Labo kits)
  • ✗ 1-2 Switch — partial incompatibility with HD Rumble-dependent mini-games
  • ✗ Games using the Game Boy / Game Boy Advance NSO games in certain controller configurations
  • ✗ Certain third-party accessories that used proprietary Joy-Con rail connections

Source: Nintendo compatibility list — check for the full updated database.

If you're unsure about a specific title, Nintendo maintains an official compatibility checker at their website where you can search by game name. For mainstream titles — any major first-party Nintendo game, any major third-party release — you will have no issues.

Do Switch 1 accessories work on Switch 2?

Partially. The Joy-Con situation is the most important thing to understand:

  • Switch 1 Joy-Cons work with Switch 2 wirelessly, but don't connect to the Switch 2's rails physically — the rail design changed. You can still use them as wireless controllers.
  • Switch 1 Pro Controllers work wirelessly with Switch 2 without any issues.
  • Switch 1 docks do not support Switch 2's 4K output. They'll work at 1080p, but you won't get the full docked experience without a Switch 2 dock.
  • Switch 1 carrying cases won't fit Switch 2 — it's slightly larger. You'll need a new case.
  • microSD cards from Switch 1 carry over completely. All your stored data and downloaded games on a microSD card work in Switch 2.

What this means if you're upgrading from Switch 1

The practical upshot: your game library is safe. Every physical cartridge you own works. Every digital purchase you made carries over through your Nintendo Account. Your save data transfers with the migration tool. You're not starting over — you're continuing your existing library on better hardware.

The things you'll want to budget for when upgrading:

  • A new carrying case — Switch 1 cases don't fit
  • A new screen protector — different screen size
  • Possibly a new dock if you want 4K output
  • Any Switch 2 Edition upgrades you want for enhanced versions of existing games

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my Switch 1 game cards in Switch 2?
Yes. The card format is identical. Insert your Switch 1 cartridge into the Switch 2's card slot and it launches normally.

Do I need to re-buy my digital games?
No. All digital purchases are tied to your Nintendo Account. Log into your account on Switch 2 and your full library appears for re-download.

Will my saves carry over?
Yes, via the data transfer tool or Nintendo Switch Online cloud saves. Our full transfer guide walks through the process step by step.

Do Switch 2 games work on Switch 1?
No. Games built specifically for Switch 2 will not run on original Switch hardware. This is the reverse of backward compatibility — Switch 2 plays Switch 1 games, but Switch 1 cannot play Switch 2 exclusive titles.

Are free enhancements automatic?
Yes. If a game has a free Switch 2 performance patch, it downloads automatically when you launch the game on Switch 2 with an internet connection.

What about Nintendo Switch Online games?
Your Nintendo Switch Online subscription carries over to Switch 2. The NES, SNES, N64, Sega Genesis, Game Boy, and GBA libraries are all accessible on Switch 2 as normal.

Bottom line

Nintendo Switch 2 backward compatibility is about as good as it gets. Your physical library works. Your digital library transfers. Your saves come with you. A handful of niche accessories and Labo titles are the only real exceptions — for a mainstream library of Switch games, compatibility is essentially universal.

If backward compatibility was the last thing holding you back from upgrading, it shouldn't be anymore. The library you've built over the past eight years is waiting for you on better hardware.
