Do you even need a second dock?

The Switch 2 ships with an official dock — so the question is whether you need an additional or replacement one. Here are the scenarios where buying another dock makes sense:

  • You want a dock in a second room — bedroom and living room both have TVs, and you don't want to unplug and carry the dock between them.
  • You travel frequently — the official dock is large and heavy. A portable dock that fits in your laptop bag means hotel room gaming without the bulk.
  • You want more USB ports — the official dock has 2× USB-A and 1× USB-C. Third-party docks often add Ethernet, extra USB-A ports, or USB-C passthrough that the official dock lacks.
  • Your official dock broke or was lost — buying a third-party replacement is significantly cheaper than purchasing an official Nintendo dock separately.
  • You want 4K but own a Switch 1 dock — Switch 1 docks cap at 1080p. Upgrading to a Switch 2-compatible dock unlocks 4K output.

What makes a dock Switch 2 compatible?

Not all docks work with Switch 2, and not all that work support 4K. Here's what to look for:

📺

HDMI 2.0 output

Required for 4K@60fps. HDMI 1.4 docks cap at 1080p. Always check the HDMI version spec before buying if 4K matters to you.

⚡

USB-C PD at 45W+

The dock must supply at least 45W via USB-C to charge the Switch 2 properly during gameplay. Lower wattage charges too slowly or not at all.

🔌

USB-A ports

For connecting wired controllers, USB drives, or a keyboard. The official dock has 2. Better third-party docks have 3. Not essential but convenient.

🌐

Ethernet port

Wired internet is faster and more stable than Wi-Fi for online gaming. Some third-party docks include Ethernet; the official Switch 2 dock does not.

⚠ Switch 1 docks and Switch 2: Your old Switch 1 dock works with Switch 2 at 1080p. It will not output 4K — the HDMI 1.4 connection inside is a hard limit. If you want 4K, you need a dock with HDMI 2.0.

Docks to avoid

✗ Any dock labeled "Switch compatible" without specifying Switch 2

Many Switch 1 docks are being sold with vague compatibility claims. If it doesn't explicitly state Switch 2 compatibility and HDMI 2.0, assume it's a Switch 1 dock — it'll cap at 1080p and may not charge at the right wattage for Switch 2's power draw during gameplay.

✗ Cheap no-name docks under $15

Sub-$15 USB-C docks claiming Switch 2 and 4K support are almost universally misrepresenting their specs. Real HDMI 2.0 hardware at that price point doesn't exist. These docks often damage the Switch 2's USB-C port over time due to incorrect power delivery implementation. The $30 Hagibis is already a budget pick — don't go lower.

✗ Switch 1 docks for 4K setups

Switch 1 docks use HDMI 1.4 which is physically incapable of 4K@60fps. Even if you enable 4K in Switch 2's system settings, it won't output through a Switch 1 dock. You'll get 1080p at best. For 4K, you need a dock with HDMI 2.0 — period.

✗ Docks with USB-C power delivery under 45W

The Switch 2 draws more power than Switch 1 during docked gameplay. A dock supplying less than 45W via USB-C will either charge slowly while gaming (losing ground on battery) or not charge at all. Some low-wattage docks also cause the Switch 2 to throttle performance to manage heat and power. 45W is the minimum — 60W+ is better.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Switch 2 dock from Nintendo work with Switch 1?
Yes — the Switch 2 dock is backward compatible and works with Switch 1 and Switch OLED at their native resolutions. If you're upgrading to Switch 2, your old games will dock and play normally through the new dock.

Can I use a USB-C to HDMI adapter instead of a dock?
Sometimes — USB-C to HDMI adapters can output video from Switch 2 but compatibility is inconsistent and most don't support 4K. They also don't charge the Switch 2 simultaneously unless they're a passthrough model. A proper dock is more reliable and charges while you play.

Why does my Switch 2 get hot in the dock?
Normal. Docked mode runs the Switch 2's GPU at full power for high-resolution output, which generates more heat than handheld mode. As long as the dock has ventilation and isn't in an enclosed cabinet, this is expected behavior. If the console shuts down from overheating, check that vents aren't blocked.

Do third-party docks void the Nintendo warranty?
Using a third-party dock doesn't void Nintendo's warranty on its own. However, damage caused by a third-party accessory — such as a dock that delivers incorrect voltage — may not be covered. Stick to reputable brands like JSAUX, Genki, and Anker to minimize this risk.

Can I connect Switch 2 to a projector through a dock?
Yes — any dock with HDMI output works with projectors that have HDMI input. For projectors with only VGA or DisplayPort, you'll need an HDMI adapter on the projector side. The Switch 2 doesn't care what's on the other end of the HDMI cable.
