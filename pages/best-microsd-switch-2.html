What microSD speed specs actually matter for Switch 2

This is where most buyers go wrong — either overpaying for extreme speeds the Switch 2 can't use, or underpaying for a card too slow to load games properly. Here's what the ratings mean and what the Switch 2 actually requires.

Speed Rating What It Means Switch 2 Compatible?
U1 / Class 10 10MB/s minimum write speed ✗ Too slow — avoid
U3 / V30 30MB/s minimum write speed ✓ Minimum required
A1 1500 random read IOPS ✓ Good for Switch 2
A2 4000 random read IOPS ✓ Best for Switch 2
100MB/s+ read Sequential read speed ✓ Minimum for fast loads
160–190MB/s read High-end sequential speed ✓ Ideal — noticeably faster loads
UHS-II 312MB/s bus speed ✗ Switch 2 only uses UHS-I — wasted money
💡 The UHS-II trap: Premium cards rated UHS-II (like some SanDisk Extreme Pro variants) cost significantly more because of their high bus speeds. The Switch 2 only supports UHS-I — it physically cannot use the extra speed. Save your money and buy a UHS-I card with fast read speeds instead.

The single most important number to look for: sequential read speed of 100MB/s or higher, paired with a U3/V30 write rating and an A2 application performance class. Every card on our list meets or exceeds this baseline.

How much storage do you actually need?

Switch 2 has 256GB of internal storage — more than Switch 1's 32GB, but Switch 2 games are also much larger. First-party titles run 10–20GB each. Demanding Switch 2 exclusives can hit 30GB+. Do the math and you'll find 256GB fills up faster than you'd expect.

Cards to avoid

These categories cause real problems — slow load times, stuttering in open-world games, or outright compatibility issues. Don't buy based on price alone.

✗ Unbranded or no-name cards (Amazon Basics excluded)

Cards without a recognized brand name — often sold under generic labels with vague specs — frequently misrepresent their speed ratings. A card labeled "100MB/s" may actually perform at 20–40MB/s. Causes long load times, game stutters, and occasional data corruption. Never worth the savings.

✗ Any card rated U1 or Class 10 without U3

U1/Class 10 means 10MB/s minimum write speed — far below what Switch 2 needs for game installations and updates. You'll see painfully slow download speeds and potential corruption during write operations. The U3/V30 rating is the minimum — don't go below it.

✗ SanDisk Ultra (not Extreme)

The SanDisk Ultra looks similar to the Extreme but is a significantly slower card — 120MB/s read and only U1 rated. It's fine for phones and cameras but falls short of Switch 2's demands for open-world games. Easy to confuse the two — double-check you're buying "Extreme" not "Ultra."

✗ UHS-II cards (overpriced for Switch 2)

Cards like the SanDisk Extreme Pro UHS-II cost significantly more because they support 312MB/s bus speeds. The Switch 2 only uses UHS-I — it caps at 104MB/s bus speed regardless of the card. You're paying for specs the console can't use. Buy UHS-I and spend the saved money on a game.

✗ Counterfeit cards sold by third-party Amazon sellers

Counterfeit SanDisk and Samsung cards are common on Amazon's marketplace. Always buy from Amazon directly (sold and fulfilled by Amazon) or from the brand's official Amazon store. If the price is suspiciously low for a name-brand card, it's likely fake — counterfeit cards often report false capacity and fail within months.

How to install a microSD card in Switch 2

The microSD slot is on the back of the Switch 2, under the kickstand. Press the kickstand open, locate the small slot, and insert the card with the label facing toward you (away from the console). Push until it clicks. The Switch 2 detects it automatically — no formatting required for new cards.

If prompted to format the card on first insertion, tap Format. This prepares the card for Switch 2's file system and takes about 30 seconds. All subsequent game downloads will use the card automatically once formatted.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a microSD card for Switch 2?
Not immediately — the 256GB internal storage is enough to get started. But with Switch 2 games averaging 15–25GB each, you'll fill the internal storage with 10–15 large games. A microSD card becomes necessary once your library grows.

Does the microSD card affect game performance?
Load times, yes. In-game performance (frame rates, resolution), no — games load from storage into RAM, so storage speed only affects how fast games load, not how they run once loaded. Faster cards mean faster load screens.

Can I use my Switch 1 microSD card in Switch 2?
Yes. Switch 2 is fully backward compatible with microSD cards from Switch 1. Your existing card and all its data work immediately — no reformatting needed.

What's the maximum microSD size Switch 2 supports?
Nintendo hasn't published an official maximum, but the Switch 2 supports the microSDXC standard which theoretically allows up to 2TB. Current 1TB cards work without issues. 2TB cards, when they become available at reasonable prices, will likely work too.

Does card brand matter?
Yes, meaningfully. SanDisk and Samsung use their own high-quality NAND and back it with long warranties. Lesser brands source NAND from multiple suppliers with inconsistent quality. Stick to SanDisk, Samsung, Lexar, or Kingston for reliability.
