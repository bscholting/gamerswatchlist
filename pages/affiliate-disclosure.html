The short version: some links on this site earn us a small commission if you buy through them. Here's how that works, what it means for our recommendations, and what it doesn't mean.

⚡ The one-paragraph version

Gamers Watchlist participates in affiliate programs including the Amazon Associates Program and others. When you click a link on this site and make a purchase, we may earn a small commission — at no extra cost to you. This commission is how the site funds itself. It does not influence which products we recommend or what we write about them. Our opinions are our own, based on genuine research and hands-on experience with the hardware and games we cover.

What affiliate links are

An affiliate link is a regular product link with a tracking code attached. When you click it and buy something — whether it's the product you clicked on or something else entirely — the retailer knows the sale came from this site and pays a small percentage of the purchase as a commission.

The price you pay is identical whether you come through an affiliate link or navigate directly to the retailer yourself. Retailers fund affiliate programs as a marketing cost. You never pay more because of a commission.

How this affects — and doesn't affect — our recommendations

Does the commission rate influence what we recommend?

No. Affiliate commission rates across Amazon product categories vary by a few percentage points at most. The difference between recommending a $30 controller and a $70 controller is a dollar or two in commission — not enough to justify recommending a worse product. Our recommendations are based on what's genuinely worth buying, not what earns the most per click.

Are products ever recommended because a company paid for placement?

No. Gamers Watchlist does not accept sponsored placements, paid reviews, or payment from manufacturers to feature their products. No company can pay to appear in a recommendation article on this site. The only revenue model is affiliate commissions on purchases made through our links.

Are review units provided by manufacturers?

Occasionally manufacturers offer review units — products sent for testing at no cost. Receipt of a review unit does not guarantee positive coverage. If a review unit is used, it will be disclosed in the relevant article. To date, all Switch 2 hardware and accessories reviewed on this site have been purchased at retail.

Why do some articles include "avoid" sections with products you don't earn commission on?

Because they're genuinely worth avoiding — and telling you that is more valuable to you than an extra commission click. A reader who buys a bad product on our recommendation and has a poor experience is a reader who never returns. Our long-term credibility is worth more than a single sale.

Do you link to products you haven't tested?

Sometimes — particularly for accessories we've researched but not personally owned. When this is the case, recommendations are based on verified specifications, aggregated user reviews, and manufacturer data rather than direct hands-on experience. We aim to be clear about this distinction in the relevant articles.

FTC compliance

The United States Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires that websites disclose material connections to companies whose products they recommend. Affiliate commissions constitute a material connection under FTC guidelines.

In accordance with those guidelines:

  • This disclosure page exists and is linked from every page on the site.
  • A brief disclosure notice ("We may earn a commission from affiliate links — at no extra cost to you") appears at the bottom of articles containing affiliate links.
  • Individual affiliate links are identified with "→" markers and contextual language throughout articles.

If you have questions about this disclosure or believe a link on this site isn't clearly identified as potentially affiliate-based, please contact us and we'll address it promptly.
