The skill gap problem — and how to solve it

The biggest challenge for couples gaming together isn’t finding a good game — it’s finding one where a skill gap doesn’t make it miserable for both players. The experienced gamer gets bored holding back. The newer player feels guilty slowing things down. The wrong game turns a fun evening into a frustrating one.

The games on this list are chosen specifically because they either eliminate the skill gap (It Takes Two, Switch Sports), scale to accommodate it gracefully (Mario Kart’s item system), or are relaxing enough that skill doesn’t really matter (Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing). These are the games couples actually enjoy rather than endure.

It Takes Two

EA / Hazelight · Rated T · Co-op Adventure · 2 players only · local or online

Best Game for Couples — Full Stop
2 players requiredLocal or online10–12 hoursGame of the YearFriend's Pass included

It Takes Two won Game of the Year because it’s the most inventively designed co-op game ever made — and it only works with two people, which means it demands genuine communication and collaboration that almost no other game achieves. Every chapter introduces a completely new mechanic that both players must master together. One chapter gives one player a magnet that attracts while the other controls one that repels. Another transforms one player into a tiny toy while the other must protect them. The creativity never stops across 12 hours of play.

The Friend’s Pass means only one person needs to buy the game — the second player downloads a free trial version and both play the full game together, locally or online. At $40 with a GOTY pedigree, it’s the most recommended purchase on this entire page.

💑 Couples note: Works equally well for gaming couples and “one gamer, one non-gamer” pairs. The mechanics are designed so neither player can progress without the other — no carry, no dead weight, no frustration from skill gaps.

Nintendo · Rated E · Racing · 1–4 players · Switch 2 exclusive

Best Competitive Pick

Mario Kart is the gold standard for couples competitive play because the item system prevents skill gaps from making races one-sided. A less experienced player trailing in last place gets better items — blue shells, lightning, bullet bills — that keep them genuinely in contention. The “one gamer, one non-gamer” cheat sheet

  • It Takes Two — the gold standard. Designed so non-gamers succeed without feeling patronized.
  • Stardew Valley — no combat, no way to fail, play at your own speed.
  • Unpacking — no controllers to learn, no enemies, no losing. Just arranging objects and telling a story.
  • Nintendo Switch Sports — motion controls remove the “I don’t know which buttons to press” barrier.
  • Avoid: Anything with a complex control scheme introduced upfront. Anything where death is frequent and restarting is required.

Frequently asked questions

Do we both need a Nintendo Switch for online co-op?
For online play across two locations, yes — each player needs their own Switch and Nintendo Switch Online subscription. For local co-op on the same couch, one Switch 2 handles both players.

What’s the best Switch 2 game if my partner has never played a video game?
It Takes Two for a shared adventure. Unpacking for no traditional gaming mechanics. Nintendo Switch Sports for something immediately physical and intuitive.

Are there Switch 2 games you’d specifically recommend NOT playing as a couple?
Any game where skill gaps create a carry dynamic. Dark Souls-style games, complex strategy games, and highly technical competitive games are better solo or with equally matched friends. For couples: co-op over competitive, shared story over individual challenge.
