Why Switch 2 is the best gaming system for kids

Nintendo has spent 40 years building a reputation for family-appropriate gaming, and the Switch 2 is the best expression of that philosophy yet. The library skews overwhelmingly toward colorful, non-violent adventures that are genuinely fun for adults too. The hybrid design means kids can play in handheld mode independently or plug into the TV for family sessions. And Nintendo's robust parental controls let parents set screen time limits, age-appropriate content filters, and restrict online play without complicated menus.

The Switch 2 is also durable — more so than previous models — with the magnetic Joy-Con connection less prone to accidental damage than the slide-rail system on Switch 1. For parents worried about a device surviving contact with an enthusiastic 6-year-old, it's the most resilient Nintendo handheld in years.

Kirby and the Forgotten Land

Nintendo · Rated E · 3D platformer · 1–2 players

Best for Young Kids
Ages 3+ Co-op available Adjustable difficulty Bright and colorful No reading required

Kirby is the perfect gateway game for very young players. The difficulty is exceptionally forgiving — Kirby floats when he falls off edges, enemies move slowly, and the "Waddle Dee Assist" mode lets a helper character automatically guide struggling players through tricky sections. A child who has never held a controller before can make meaningful progress within minutes of starting.

The 3D adventure is colorful, cheerful, and entirely violence-free in any meaningful sense — Kirby inhales enemies and copies their abilities, which young children find endlessly delightful. A second player can join as Bandana Waddle Dee at any point, making it a parent-child co-op experience that genuinely works even when skill levels are wildly mismatched.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent note: No online multiplayer, no in-app purchases, no dark or frightening themes. Content is entirely appropriate for toddlers. The assist mode means you won't need to take over the controller when your 4-year-old gets stuck — the game handles it gracefully.

Yoshi's Crafted World

Nintendo · Rated E · Platformer · 1–2 players

Ages 3+
Ages 3+ Co-op available Mellow difficulty Creative art style Exploration focus

Yoshi's Crafted World takes place in a world made of cardboard, yarn, and everyday objects — an art style that young children respond to immediately and enthusiastically. The platforming is gentle and exploratory rather than challenging, with hidden collectibles that reward curious players who look around levels rather than rushing through.

The co-op mode lets two players share the experience with one riding on Yoshi's back — perfect for a parent and very young child playing together where the parent handles the hard parts naturally by being in control. Mellow difficulty means no frustration and no scary moments.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent note: One of the least stressful games available for young kids. No timers, no fail states that feel punishing, and a visual style that feels like an animated arts and crafts project. Excellent for ages 3–6.

Mario Kart World

Nintendo · Rated E · Racing · 1–4 players local · online multiplayer

Best Family Game
Ages 5+ 4-player local Multiple difficulty modes Switch 2 exclusive Online play

Mario Kart is the single best multiplayer game for mixed-age family sessions and Mario Kart World, the Switch 2 launch title, is the best version yet. Four players can race simultaneously on one TV, the item system means a last-place player can still get a lucky blue shell win, and the 150cc/200cc difficulty modes let adults and kids play at their own speed simultaneously.

The open-world driving mode is new to this entry — between races, players can freely drive through a massive connected world, finding hidden items and shortcuts. For kids who just want to drive around without racing pressure, it's a low-stakes sandbox that younger children find genuinely absorbing for extended periods.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent note: Online multiplayer exists but can be disabled entirely through parental controls. Local play only is a completely viable experience with the game's enormous offline content. No in-game purchases. Rated E for Everyone.

Super Mario Bros. Wonder

Nintendo · Rated E · 2D Platformer · 1–4 players

Ages 5+
Ages 5+ Up to 4-player co-op Creative level design Multiple characters Adjustable difficulty

Super Mario Bros. Wonder is the most inventive 2D Mario game in a generation — each level contains a Wonder Flower that transforms the rules of that level entirely, creating constantly surprising moments that delight kids and adults alike. Up to four players can play simultaneously, with a Nabbit character who cannot be hurt by enemies — perfect for younger or less experienced players who want to participate without the frustration of dying repeatedly.

The level variety is extraordinary — no two levels feel alike, and the Wonder sequences create genuinely memorable "did that really just happen" moments that kids will talk about for days. One of the best pure platformers Nintendo has ever made, fully backward compatible with Switch 2 and enhanced for higher resolution.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent note: The Nabbit/Yoshi characters who can't take damage are specifically designed for younger players. Four-player co-op means the whole family can play together. No online multiplayer, no purchases, nothing concerning for parents.

Pikmin 4

Nintendo · Rated E · Strategy/Adventure · 1 player

Educational Pick
Ages 6+ Problem-solving focus Nature themes Low-stress gameplay Creative thinking

Pikmin 4 is a gentle strategy game where players command small plant-animal creatures to solve puzzles, explore environments, and rescue survivors. It's one of the few games that develops genuine problem-solving and resource management thinking in an age-appropriate package — kids learn to think ahead, manage groups, and prioritize tasks, all through a lens of colorful characters and nature exploration.

The Dandori challenge levels introduce time-management concepts in a low-stakes format. The night missions are slightly more tense but never scary. The overall tone is warm and curious — Pikmin 4 feels like a nature documentary you can play. For kids who enjoy thoughtful play over action, it's exceptional.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent note: Pikmin do "die" in the game — small plant creatures can be lost to enemies. Most children handle this without distress, but very sensitive kids around age 5–6 may find it upsetting. Age 7+ is a safer entry point for sensitive players.

The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom

Nintendo · Rated E10+ · Action Adventure · 1 player

Best Ages 8–12
Ages 8+ Play as Zelda Puzzle-adventure Creative problem solving E10+ rating

Echoes of Wisdom lets players control Princess Zelda for the first time in the series' history — a meaningful change that many kids respond to enthusiastically. The gameplay centers on copying and placing objects and creatures to solve puzzles creatively, meaning there's rarely one correct solution and kids are rewarded for imaginative thinking. The adventure spans a large world with dungeons, secrets, and a genuinely engaging story.

The E10+ rating reflects mild fantasy combat and slightly more complex narrative themes than the E-rated games above — appropriate for confident readers and players who've outgrown simpler titles. For kids aged 8–10 who want something with real depth and challenge, this is the best Nintendo has to offer.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent note: E10+ for Fantasy Violence and Mild Suggestive Themes — the violence is strictly cartoon sword-fighting with no blood or disturbing content. The game features a female protagonist, which many parents note their daughters respond to positively. Requires basic reading ability.

Pokémon needs no introduction — it's the best-selling media franchise in history and for good reason. What parents should know before buying

🔒

Parental controls are excellent

The Nintendo Switch 2's parental controls app lets you set screen time limits, restrict content by age rating, disable online play, and monitor activity — all from your phone. Set these up before handing the console to your child.

🛒

Disable eShop purchases

Add a PIN to the eShop in parental controls before your child plays. Without this, accidental purchases of digital games are possible. Takes 30 seconds to set up and prevents unexpected charges.

🌐

Online play is optional in all these games

Every game on this list is fully enjoyable without online connectivity. Online features can be disabled entirely through parental controls or by simply not setting up Nintendo Switch Online.

💰

Physical games hold resale value

Nintendo physical games retain value unusually well. A $60 physical game your child stops playing can often be resold for $35–$45. Digital purchases cannot be resold. For kids who move through games quickly, physical is worth the premium.

👨‍👩‍👧

Co-op is better with the right controller

For family co-op, each player needs a controller. The Joy-Cons can be used sideways as individual controllers for 2-player sessions. For 3–4 players, additional Joy-Con sets or a pro controller are needed.

📱

Nintendo Switch Online for online play

Online multiplayer in any game requires a Nintendo Switch Online subscription ($20/year individual, $35/year family plan covering up to 8 accounts). The family plan is worth it if multiple family members will use the console.

Frequently asked questions

What age is the Nintendo Switch 2 appropriate for?
Nintendo recommends the Switch 2 for ages 6 and up for general use. With appropriate game selection (Kirby, Yoshi) and parental supervision, children as young as 3–4 can enjoy it meaningfully. The hardware is durable but not indestructible — a carrying case is recommended if young children will handle it frequently.

Are there educational games on Switch 2?
Yes — Pikmin 4 develops problem-solving and resource management. Big Brain Academy: Brain vs. Brain targets memory, math, and pattern recognition. Endless Ocean Luminous teaches marine biology through exploration. Nintendo Labo (Switch 1, backward compatible) teaches basic engineering and coding concepts.

Can kids play Switch 2 games they already own on Switch 1?
Yes — Switch 2 is fully backward compatible with Switch 1 games. Every game your child owns on Switch 1 works on Switch 2. Save data can be transferred so they continue exactly where they left off. See our data transfer guide →

What's the best starter game for a child who has never played a video game?
Kirby and the Forgotten Land. The assist mode, forgiving difficulty, and immediately joyful gameplay make it the most accessible entry point for a complete beginner of any age. After Kirby, Super Mario Bros. Wonder is the natural next step.

Is Minecraft on Switch 2?
Yes — Minecraft is available on Switch 2 and runs with improved performance compared to Switch 1. It's one of the most popular games for kids aged 7–12 and is sold separately from the games listed above. Purchase through the eShop or as a physical cartridge.
