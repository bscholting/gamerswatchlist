Do you actually need a pro controller?

The Joy-Cons that come with Switch 2 are capable controllers — genuinely better than they were on Switch 1 with the magnetic attachment and improved analog sticks. For casual gaming, party games, and handheld play, they're perfectly fine.

A pro controller becomes worthwhile when:

  • You play docked for extended sessions — holding Joy-Cons individually or in a grip for 2+ hours causes hand fatigue for most adults. A full-size controller eliminates this.
  • You play competitive games — fighting games, shooters, and sports titles benefit from the ergonomics and button layout of a traditional controller.
  • You play with others — having a second full controller means a second player gets a proper gaming experience instead of a single detached Joy-Con.
  • You're experiencing Joy-Con drift — if your sticks are registering phantom inputs, a pro controller bypasses the problem entirely.

What to look for in a Switch 2 pro controller

🎯

Hall effect sticks

Magnetic sensors instead of mechanical potentiometers — immune to drift. Worth paying more for if you've ever dealt with stick drift.

🔋

Battery life

Look for 20+ hours per charge. The official Pro Controller leads at ~40 hours. Anything under 15 hours becomes inconvenient fast.

📶

Wireless vs wired

Wireless is more convenient. Wired has zero input lag — matters for competitive fighting games. Most players will never notice wired vs wireless latency.

🎮

Rumble & gyro

Nintendo games use HD Rumble and gyroscope aiming extensively. Third-party controllers vary — check each one's rumble and gyro support before buying.

⚙️

Remappable buttons

Back paddles and remappable buttons help in competitive games. Premium feature — not essential for casual play but genuinely useful for dedicated gamers.

✋

Ergonomics

Switch 2 Pro Controller shape is comfortable for large and medium hands. 8BitDo skews slightly smaller. Kids controllers need a smaller grip.

A note on Joy-Con drift and hall effect sticks

Joy-Con drift — where the analog sticks register movement without being touched — has been Nintendo's most persistent hardware criticism for years. The Switch 2 Joy-Cons use revised analog sticks that Nintendo claims are more drift-resistant, but they still use the same basic potentiometer mechanism that caused drift in Switch 1.

Hall effect sticks, used in the 8BitDo Ultimate 2 Hall Effect Edition, use magnets instead of physical contact — they literally cannot drift because there's nothing to wear out. If drift has caused you problems before, the hall effect controller is worth the price premium as a long-term investment.

Frequently asked questions

Do Switch 1 Pro Controllers work on Switch 2?
Yes — the Switch 1 Pro Controller pairs wirelessly with Switch 2 and works for most games. It won't support Switch 2-specific features like the new GameChat button, but for standard gameplay it functions normally. If you already own one, no need to replace it unless you want the Switch 2-specific version.

Can I use an Xbox or PlayStation controller on Switch 2?
Not natively — they don't pair wirelessly with Switch 2 out of the box. Third-party adapters like the Mayflash Magic-NS allow Xbox and PS controllers to connect, but with limitations. 8BitDo also makes adapters for this purpose. Not a clean solution — easier to buy a Switch-native controller.

Does the Pro Controller charge while the Switch 2 is in sleep mode?
Yes. Connect the Pro Controller to the Switch 2 dock via USB-C and it charges whether the console is on, sleeping, or off (as long as the dock is powered).

Is the 8BitDo controller officially supported by Nintendo?
8BitDo is not officially licensed by Nintendo, but their controllers are designed to be compatible and work reliably. The main features that require Nintendo licensing — Amiibo NFC — are absent from 8BitDo controllers. Everything else works.

How do I connect a pro controller to Switch 2?
For wireless: HOME → Controllers → Change Grip/Order → press the sync button on the controller (small button near the top). For wired: plug a USB-C cable from the controller into the dock or directly into the Switch 2. The console detects it automatically.
